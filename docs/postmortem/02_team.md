# Project 01 Post Mortem - Group 2, Cst438Project-01

## [Link to repo](https://github.com/bob234-ui/Cst438Project-01)

## Context
We set out to build an FBI Wanted List informational app, and for the most part, that’s what we shipped. We had user-specific data in the form of favorited suspects, a search screen where users could find specific suspects, and a favorites page where users could keep track of subjects of their choosing. We wanted to be more ambitious, with more detailed reports on specific subjects and categorical sorting, but not all of it was implemented.

## By the numbers
- Issues opened: 14 (https://github.com/bob234-ui/Cst438Project-01/issues) | closed: 20
- Pull requests opened: 16 (https://github.com/bob234-ui/Cst438Project-01/pulls) | merged: 16
- Planned at kickoff: 5 stories | done: 4

## What went well
1. Time-oriented check-ins forced us to keep moving forward, even if only a small amount of progress was made
2. Having issues small enough that we were able to complete them in a week, we were able to build off each other's work faster.
3. Good Git hygiene, as in every feature and every change had its own branch, committing often and early, drafting PRs, and protecting main. 

## What went wrong
1. Misintegration - Cause: Not implementing comments/tests from the start made backtracking harder to do.
2. Having Vague issues: at first it wasn’t a problem, then an issue got misinterpreted, and we didn’t have enough time to fix it.
3. Poor time management - Working on issues at the last minute can lead to confusion, misunderstanding, little time to go back and revise, and/or failure.

## Advice to our next teams
1. Don’t forget about the comments. Seriously.
2. Make sure the issues are clear so everyone can understand what is being asked for in those issues.
3. If you have the time, start early; bugs and troubleshooting are not fun to deal with late. Coding early = less groggy = better code
